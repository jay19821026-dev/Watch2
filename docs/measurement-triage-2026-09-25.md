## Research basis for safe usable features

- Wearable PPG can support pulse-rate work only with sampling configuration, contact/motion handling, and signal-quality gating established for the actual stream; HRV/PRV is not interchangeable with ECG HRV under all conditions. See the peer-reviewed [2023 wearable PPG roadmap](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10686289/) and [wearable PPG cardiovascular-monitoring review](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7612541/).
- Smartwatch single-lead ECG screening results are device- and study-protocol-specific; Apple Heart Study alerts were followed by confirmatory ECG patch assessment, not treated as definitive diagnosis. See [NEJM Apple Heart Study](https://www.nejm.org/doi/full/10.1056/NEJMoa1901183) and a [single-lead smartwatch ECG accuracy review](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9795256/).
- Consumer sleep wearables do not replace PSG for sleep staging. AASM's 2024 review reports poor performance for many consumer devices versus PSG, reinforcing that sleep duration/trend and measured sleep stages must not be conflated. See [Sleep, 2024 review](https://sleepresearchsociety.org/wp-content/uploads/2024/08/zsad325.pdf).
- Suitable near-term app features from available validated inputs are transparent trends and conventional arithmetic (step totals, distance/elevation trends, HR-zone time only when real BPM and user profile exist, and resting-HR trend with source/freshness). Do not enable AFib diagnosis, cuffless blood pressure, SpO₂, BioZ body composition, sleep-stage, apnea, or disease prediction without a validated device signal and application-specific clinical validation.

## Literature review notes for metric validity

- **PPG / PRV:** The 2023 wearable PPG roadmap identifies motion, placement and signal quality as major sources of unusable or misleading readings and recommends explicit SQI, acquisition protocols and device/population validation ([Ding et al., *The 2023 wearable photoplethysmography roadmap*](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10686289/)). A 2023 systematic review of wearable HRV during COVID-era monitoring notes that PPG-derived variability is best supported at rest or low activity and degrades as exercise load increases ([JMIR review](https://www.jmir.org/2023/1/e47112)). For this watch's unmapped 16-channel, clockless HAL payload, this supports preserving the raw event and rejecting BPM/HRV derivation; neither paper supplies the Xiaomi channel map or conversion.
- **BioZ / body composition:** A 186-participant smartwatch BIA comparison against a four-compartment reference found the watch BIA was not equivalent to the reference for body-fat estimates and reported sex-dependent errors ([study record, PubMed 36462216](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/36462216/)). It is evidence against borrowing another manufacturer's impedance coefficients, not a calibration source for MAX86178 data on Xiaomi.
- **Temperature:** The systematic review found 20 studies and 25 algorithms for estimating core temperature from wearable signals; algorithms use different combinations of skin temperature, heart rate and environmental/contextual inputs ([Dolson et al., 2022](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9572283/)). A bare wrist/board-temperature stream therefore cannot be relabelled as core temperature or fever without a device-specific, externally validated model.
- **Sleep staging:** The 2024 systematic review identified only eight wrist-actigraphy versus PSG studies and found substantial heterogeneity in stage grouping and performance metrics ([Smith et al., *Journal of Sleep Research*](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7618292/)). Accelerometer plus HRV heuristics can at most show an explicitly estimated trend; they cannot provide PSG-equivalent deep/light/REM diagnosis.
- **Fall risk:** The 2022 review found most included risk-assessment studies used accelerometer+gyroscope IMUs and commonly placed them nearer the trunk/lower body rather than wrist ([systematic review](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9504041/)). A wrist-only result should remain a movement/fall-event heuristic, not a validated clinical fall-risk score.
- **Cuffless BP:** A systematic review/meta-analysis describes a dearth of device validity and no consensus validation standard for cuffless devices ([Picone et al.](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9708022/)). ECG/PPG timing or BioZ features alone cannot be used to emit systolic/diastolic BP without watch-specific calibration and independent validation.

These studies guide honest feature scope and signal gates; none substitutes for Xiaomi/OEM firmware documentation, ground-truth paired measurements, or clinical validation. As a result, software can reduce avoidable capture/pipeline failures and make research trends useful, but cannot guarantee zero failures or 100% medical accuracy.
