## Evidence boundary

The chip names below are the app's current routing labels in `WearRawSensorPublisher.chipFor`; they are not a verified Xiaomi Watch 2 Pro bill of materials. A `stringType`, vendor string, or chip-family label does not establish the physical component, signal units, sampling clock, calibration, or clinical validity. Confirm physical part numbers against Xiaomi documentation or a matching board/HAL inventory before describing them as verified.

## Research-backed priorities

1. **Signal quality before pulse inference.** A systematic review/meta-analysis of wrist PPG found accuracy varies by activity and device; use contact, motion, saturation, timestamp continuity, and protocol-specific gates. Do not treat arbitrary MAX86178 ADC values as bpm or clinical SpO₂. Zhang et al., *Validity of Wrist-Worn Photoplethysmography Devices to Measure Heart Rate: A Systematic Review and Meta-analysis* (2020), https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/32552580/.
2. **Sleep/wake trends before sleep-stage claims.** Actigraphy is useful for movement/sleep-wake patterns, but stage estimates are not PSG. Yuan et al., *A systematic review of the performance of actigraphy in measuring sleep stages* (2024), https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/38384163/.
3. **Fall alerts need device/user validation.** Published wearable fall-risk systems use heterogeneous placements, activities, definitions, and validation; an acceleration threshold is not a clinical risk score. See *Wearable Sensor Systems for Fall Risk Assessment: A Review* (2022), https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/35911615/ and the umbrella review https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8591794/.
4. **Energy expenditure and step counts need error bounds.** Consumer activity-tracker validity varies substantially across metrics and protocols; present estimates with source, context, and uncertainty rather than false precision. Systematic review, https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/32897239/.

## MAX86178 PPG/HRV evidence review — 2026-09-25

- The Analog Devices MAX86178 Rev. 4 datasheet describes two optical readout channels, up to six LEDs/four photodiode inputs, a 256-word FIFO, and FIFO timing data for synchronized PPG/ECG/BioZ. These are AFE capabilities, not evidence that the Xiaomi watch exposes those channel identities, timing tags, sample rate, LED drive settings, or calibrated values through its Android Sensor HAL. The app's observed 16-value HAL payload therefore remains unmapped until Xiaomi supplies the watch-side configuration or a verified controlled capture maps it. Datasheet: https://www.analog.com/media/en/technical-documentation/data-sheets/max86178.pdf.
- Orphanidou et al. describe signal-quality indices as a prerequisite for deciding whether wearable ECG/PPG can yield reliable heart-rate estimates; use explicit quality gating rather than reducing the gate just to force nonempty output. *IEEE Journal of Biomedical and Health Informatics* (2015), doi:10.1109/JBHI.2014.2338351, https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/25069129/.
- Georgiou et al.'s systematic review found wearable HRV agreement was strongest at rest and declined as exercise intensity increased; the review also noted limited PPG-PRV evidence at that time. Keep PPG-derived PRV distinct from ECG-derived HRV and label it research-only unless validated for this watch and protocol. *Folia Med (Plovdiv)* (2018), doi:10.2478/folmed-2018-0012, https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/29668452/.
- Implementation consequence: unknown HAL sample timing/channel mapping means no credible PPG beat-to-beat series can be reconstructed from event arrival cadence. Retain waveform/raw diagnostics, show the exact missing prerequisite, and continue using separately timestamped Health Services BPM where available; never synthesize timing or treat PRV as ECG HRV.

## One-family-at-a-time validation protocol

For each family, run a user-started, bounded capture while worn and off charger. Record the app session ID, selected HAL sensor name/vendor/string type, registration result, first/last event timestamps, event count, requested period, payload length, quality rejection reason, final Data Layer enqueue request, phone accepted snapshot count, and persisted measurement time. Run one family first, then repeat under controlled stillness/motion/contact conditions. Compare only against an appropriate independent reference instrument; do not infer missing calibration from a similar chip or another Xiaomi product.

Release acceptance requires a fresh on-device export that demonstrates the whole chain; unit tests and successful APK assembly are not hardware evidence. Zero failure probability and 100% medical accuracy are not achievable guarantees for consumer wearable sensors.

## 2026-09-26 follow-up: power and useful outputs

- Android's current Wear OS Health Services guidance separates low-frequency, long-lived background collection (`PassiveMonitoringClient`) from short-lived UI measurements (`MeasureClient`); it explicitly warns that keeping a Measure listener registered increases sampling rate and power. Use passive Health Services for supported everyday HR/steps, and make raw HAL discovery/PPG/ECG/BioZ bounded, explicitly user-started foreground captures. Passive delivery is batched and not at a predictable interval, so the UI must show the metric timestamp rather than imply a missing callback means sensor failure. Sources: [Health Services overview](https://developer.android.com/health-and-fitness/health-services), [Health Services compatibility and batching](https://developer.android.com/health-and-fitness/health-services/compatibility), [power guidance](https://developer.android.com/training/wearables/apps/power).
- Charlton et al.'s 2023 PPG roadmap supports treating motion artefact and signal-quality assessment as first-class prerequisites; the useful output for this watch today is quality, coverage, contact and provenance, not a guessed clinical unit. [PubMed 37494945](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/37494945/).
- No low-power code change is made solely from stale BatteryStats: historical counters cannot identify present drain. The old build's broad-suite partial wake lock is bounded/released in normal, cancel, deadline and destroy paths; next power comparison must log scan duration and battery before/after under the same watch state, while counting samples accepted per channel.
- Next implementation gate: select a single channel family from `docs/watch-chip-inventory.md`, run the controlled one-family capture, and only then patch the stage that evidence identifies. For each failed reading, maintain one primary class (not exposed / registration / no event or unmet condition / transport / persistence / stale / quality / raw unscaled) and preserve the last valid sample as stale rather than synthesizing a replacement.

## 2026-09-26 follow-up: clinical-use literature and safe app candidates

- **Wrist PPG / SpO₂ validity:** Singh et al., *Impact of Skin Pigmentation on Pulse Oximetry Blood Oxygenation and Wearable Pulse Rate Accuracy: Systematic Review and Meta-Analysis* (JMIR, 2024; [PubMed 39388258](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/39388258/)). Their pooled evidence reports substantial limits of agreement for wearable pulse rate and SpO₂ accuracy metrics that can exceed common regulatory thresholds. App implication: preserve sensor/source/contact context, expose measurement quality and timestamp, and avoid presenting raw HAL integers as a percent or a clinical pulse-ox result. This paper does not validate this Xiaomi device.
- **Sleep stages:** *A systematic review of the performance of actigraphy in measuring sleep stages* (2024; [PubMed 38384163](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/38384163/)) compared wrist actigraphy to polysomnography in eight studies. App implication: actigraphy-only sleep/wake regularity and duration trends are supportable as estimates; REM/deep/light staging should remain explicitly exploratory without PSG-like reference validation. Do not invent stage percentages when required inputs are absent.
- **Fall-risk classification:** *Performance and Characteristics of Wearable Sensor Systems Discriminating and Classifying Older Adults According to Fall Risk: A Systematic Review* (2021; [PubMed 34502755](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/34502755/)) found promising results in some studies but also small samples, insufficient prospective designs, inconsistent features and limited validation. *Are wearable devices effective for preventing and detecting falls: an umbrella review* (2021; [PubMed 34775947](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/34775947/)) likewise calls for better real-world evidence in frail older adults. App implication: inertial sensors can support an explainable fall-event candidate and user-confirmation flow; do not market it as guaranteed fall detection or validated individual fall probability without prospective Watch 2 Pro data.
- **SpO₂ optical bias:** Shi et al., *The accuracy of pulse oximetry in measuring oxygen saturation by levels of skin pigmentation: a systematic review and meta-analysis* (BMC Medicine, 2022; [PubMed 35971142](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/35971142/)) reports pigmentation-related bias/precision concerns even for clinical pulse oximetry. Together with the wearable-specific review above, this reinforces that software post-scaling cannot replace device-specific calibration and representative reference testing.
- **Implementation prioritization:** immediate health value with available trustworthy sources is data provenance/recency, passive Health Services HR and activity summaries, PPG signal-quality/contact/coverage diagnostics, skin-temperature trend (not core temperature), and inertial activity summaries. Derived clinical outputs (AF, sleep-stage diagnoses, SpO₂ disease alerts, fall-risk probability) need suitable device-specific reference datasets and prospective validation; literature alone does not calibrate the watch.
- These sources were reviewed for direction and limitations only. No disease classifier or new physiological conversion was added from paper-level results alone.

- **Health Services batching nuance (official docs, 2026-09-26):** `PassiveMonitoringClient` provides batched, device-dependent updates with no predictable interval; compatibility guidance notes some watches can produce HR points about once per second. This describes delivered sample cadence, not an app guarantee or proof of battery drain. [Compatibility and batching](https://developer.android.com/health-and-fitness/health-services/compatibility), [PassiveMonitoringClient API](https://developer.android.com/reference/androidx/health/services/client/PassiveMonitoringClient). The app's selected interval is only a downstream transfer gate; it cannot request the watch sensor hub to take readings at that exact cadence.

- **Sensor timestamp contract and current PPG triage (2026-09-26):** Android `SensorEvent.timestamp` is the sensor event time in nanoseconds; successive events for one sensor should be monotonically increasing on the `elapsedRealtimeNanos()` time base ([official API](https://developer.android.com/reference/android/hardware/SensorEvent)). A HAL `unorder sample` line merits checking, but by itself does not prove the app dropped data or that the internal AFE waveform clock is known. The current paired log proves 128 PPG raw points reached and were stored by the phone as `research_signal_unscaled`; it does not establish calibrated pulse rate/HRV/SpO₂. Event-to-event cadence must not be equated with ADC sample frequency when each event may pack multiple unmapped values.
- **PPG signal quality evidence:** an integrative review explains that low-quality PPG can create analysis errors and false alarms, that motion/contact/ambient light affect usability, and reviews quality indices including morphology, perfusion, entropy and template matching ([review, PMC8920970](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8920970/)). Elgendi et al. compared eight PPG quality indices against expert labels on 106 60-second recordings; skewness best separated excellent/acceptable/unfit in that dataset, with F1 dependent on the classification grouping, not a universal device accuracy guarantee ([Optimal Signal Quality Index for Photoplethysmogram Signals](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/28952584/)). A separate supervised SQI study cautions that pulse-template similarity alone can fail on AF morphology and reports evaluation on a small 13-patient stroke subset plus a larger ICU set ([PMC9553283](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9553283/)). These studies support transparent quality flags and reference validation, not diagnosing AF from this watch's unknown raw channels.
- **Fresh device-specific PPG failure evidence (2026-09-26):** the phone export for the paired 03:52 capture reports 8 of 8 `SensorEvent` timestamps invalid, no measurable inter-event intervals, 128 unscaled raw points, and quality score 25/100; the app nevertheless received and stored the matching 128-point snapshot. Android's timestamp contract requires same-sensor events to increase monotonically on the `elapsedRealtimeNanos()` base ([official API](https://developer.android.com/reference/android/hardware/SensorEvent)). Therefore the demonstrated failure is not phone transport; it is invalid source timing plus unverified units/quality, which blocks time-based pulse/HRV output. Do not replace invalid times with callback time or requested period.
- **More recent PPG SQA evidence (2025):** a custom headband study acquired PPG plus accelerometry from 54 participants and reports 95.6% test accuracy for random-forest SQA over its own split/annotations ([PubMed 41308204](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/41308204/), DOI 10.1088/2057-1976/ae250f). Hardware, participants, labels and test protocol differ from Watch 2 Pro; this does not validate the app's current heuristic or justify a medical confidence score.
- **PPG AF evidence boundary (2024):** a real-world study in 50 already-diagnosed AF patients compared fingertip smartphone PPG with handheld ECG; only 3,407/3,907 paired measurements had consistent ECG diagnosis and adequate PPG quality, and repeated PPG improved signal quality from 89.5% to 96.6% ([PubMed 38630867](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/38630867/)). This specific population/device/protocol cannot be transferred to unknown wrist PPG channels or used as validation of this app's AF diagnosis. Repeated measurement is a reasonable user-facing retry strategy, not a guarantee of 100% usable data.
- **Wearable SpO₂ validation and bias (2024):** a systematic review/meta-analysis of pulse oximetry and wearable pulse-rate studies included 23 SpO₂ studies (59,684 participants) and four wearable-PR studies (176 participants); pooled results found accuracy/limits-of-agreement concerns and uneven skin-pigmentation representation. These heterogeneous devices do not validate Watch 2 Pro, but reinforce that raw optical channels cannot be converted to clinical SpO₂ without device-specific calibration and representative reference testing ([Singh et al., JMIR 2024, PMID 39388258](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/39388258/)).
- **Consumer HR validation design (INTERLIVE, 2021):** the expert statement/checklist emphasizes protocol, comparator, participant/activity characteristics, and reporting of agreement rather than correlation alone. Apply this to any later Watch 2 Pro HR validation; a high internal signal-quality score is not accuracy evidence ([INTERLIVE Network, PMID 33397674](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/33397674/)).

PPG still has 8/8 invalid event timestamps and fill-heavy packet warning; SpO₂ raw, ECG, BioZ, and temperature remain uncalibrated/non-diagnostic. This independently reproduces the earlier PPG timing/quality blocker and supersedes the old 03:58 stale-data snapshot.

## Additional evidence reviewed (2026-09-27)

- **Cross-device validation is not portable:** the 2024 living umbrella review of consumer wearables included 24 systematic reviews and 653 underlying studies, yet found only 34 of 310 listed device models validated for at least one biometric outcome; protocols, populations, outcomes and reference standards were highly heterogeneous. Do not copy Apple/Samsung thresholds or accuracy claims to Xiaomi hardware ([Doherty et al., Sports Medicine, 2024](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s40279-024-02077-2)).
- **SpO₂ needs endpoint/device validation:** a 2023 consumer-watch study enrolled 49 people and compared outputs with a reference pulse oximeter; one device (Apple Watch Series 7) had only 58.3% of readings within the study's ±2% reference range and also overestimated readings. This is not an estimate for Xiaomi; it demonstrates why raw HAL values or another vendor's algorithm cannot establish usable SpO₂ ([Jiang et al., PLOS Digital Health, 2023](https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pdig.0000296)). The separate 2024 systematic review documented sparse/uneven pigmentation representation and accuracy concerns across heterogeneous pulse oximeters/wearables ([Singh et al., JMIR, 2024, PMID 39388258](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/39388258/)).
- **Bioimpedance comparator study is model-specific:** a study of Samsung wrist BIA watches compared estimates with DXA and field BIA; the watch method differed from DXA, and its procedure depended on the watch electrodes, hand contact and a completed measurement. It provides no transferable Xiaomi Ω-to-body-composition equation ([study, PMC](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11530365/)).
- **AF performance does not transfer to an uncalibrated signal:** a 2025 smartwatch meta-analysis pooled 26 studies / 17,349 patients and reported high aggregate sensitivity/specificity, but heterogeneity was very high (I² 95%/99%); model, algorithm, usable-trace handling and reference protocol matter. This does not validate this watch's ECG/PPG or authorize disease diagnosis ([JACC: Advances, 2025](https://www.jacc.org/doi/full/10.1016/j.jacadv.2025.102133)).
- **Chip documentation boundary:** Analog Devices describes MAX86178 AFE capabilities and its EV kit/reference designs (including configured LEDs, photodiodes, electrodes, MCU/firmware and algorithms). Those board-level designs do not establish Xiaomi's actual electrode routing, optical configuration, register settings, sample mapping, units or calibration. Manufacturer chip data can inform a raw-channel decoder only after the watch's full signal chain is evidenced; it cannot be substituted for OEM calibration ([MAX86178 product/data sheet](https://www.analog.com/en/products/max86178.html), [MAX86178 EV-kit guide](https://www.analog.com/media/en/technical-documentation/user-guides/max86178evkit.pdf), [MAXREFDES106 reference platform](https://www.analog.com/en/resources/reference-designs/maxrefdes106.html)).
- **Implementation consequence:** prioritize same-session acquisition diagnostics, per-channel event timestamps, quality/provenance and readable raw-only status. No disease classifier or physiological conversion is enabled by this literature review alone; exact-watch validation against suitable reference instruments is still required.
