# Wearable health algorithms: evidence and implementation gates

## Decision

Build useful outputs only after proving the acquisition and units for that channel. The immediate highest-value algorithm is **signal/data quality and provenance**, not disease classification: it reduces bad outputs, distinguishes a real no-event from a failed pipeline, and prevents old measurements being mistaken for new ones.

The latest paired UI scan is 2026-09-27 09:58–10:01 on installed v3.13.15/code125: 24/34 stages returned samples, 26/36 channels had raw events, 18 were fresh at export, and 0 physiological rows passed the signal-quality gate. Every event-bearing suite channel had one accepted snapshot on the phone, so this run does not support a broad Data Layer/storage failure. PPG had 8/8 missing event timestamps and no HAL sample clock; the optical, SpO₂, ECG and BioZ raw packets still lack device-specific mapping/units/calibration. A separate Health Services heart-rate sample was fresh at export and can follow the app's timestamped single-reading display path; the raw HR sensor events were rejected. The off-body detector disagreed with wearer confirmation; this remains an unresolved sensor/contact inconsistency, not a clinical conclusion. This partial scan is not an accuracy study. ## Research findings

1. **PPG quality gating — implement as research SQI, not diagnosis.** PPG processing literature explicitly treats motion artifact, baseline wander, hypoperfusion, preprocessing, peak detection, and signal-quality indexing as separate problems; no single standardized preprocessing pipeline applies to all acquisition methods. A literature survey of 118 papers is useful for feature candidates, not a transferable device calibration. [Integrative PPG review, 2022](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/35300400/) · [PPG/iPPG quality assessment survey, 2022](https://www.mdpi.com/2076-3417/12/19/9582)
2. **PRV is not automatically ECG HRV.** A recent systematic review/meta-analysis found PPG-derived PRV agreement estimates mainly under selected resting/controlled conditions and warned against generalizing them to sleep, exercise, stress, or free-living use. The older wearable-HRV review also reports heterogeneous agreement. [PRV vs ECG-HRV meta-analysis](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42655500/) · [Wearable HRV systematic review](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/29668452/) · [1996 HRV measurement standards](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/8598068/)
3. **Sleep actigraphy supports sleep/wake estimates, not PSG stage equivalence.** AASM guidance describes actigraphy as estimating sleep parameters; PSG remains the direct reference. Do not create deep/light/REM stages or apnea diagnosis from accelerometer alone. [AASM actigraphy guideline](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6040807/) · [AASM actigraphy practice parameters](https://aasm.org/resources/practiceparameters/pp_actigraphy_update.pdf)
4. **Smartwatch ECG does not support unrestricted arrhythmia diagnosis.** The 2025 JHRS/JCS consensus says smartwatch ECG can aid evaluation but must not be used alone to diagnose arrhythmia; movement and poor fit produce noise. It also distinguishes PPG screening from ECG confirmation. [2025 JHRS/JCS consensus](https://doi.org/10.1002/joa3.70059)
5. **Wrist HR accuracy depends on activity.** A meta-analysis of 44 studies across 15 brands found small mean differences during sleep, rest, and treadmill walking/running, but larger errors for resistance exercise and cycling; this supports contextual HR trends, not assuming equal accuracy across activities. [Wrist-worn PPG HR meta-analysis](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/32552580/)
6. **Energy expenditure is not a clinically precise watch output.** A systematic review of 65 wrist-tracker studies found high heterogeneity and reported energy-expenditure MAPE above 30% across tested brands; active calories should be labelled a model estimate and validated against an appropriate reference before being used for coaching/medical conclusions. [Wrist activity-tracker accuracy review](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/35060915/)
7. **Sleep stages need device-specific PSG validation.** A scoping review of 35 studies and 62 wearable configurations found broad variation in sensors, features, algorithms, and evaluation methods. Use this literature to design PSG-subset validation; do not transfer another brand's sleep-stage classifier or treat actigraphy as deep/light/REM ground truth. [Wearable sleep-staging reliability review](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/38499793/)
8. **Published smartwatch SpO₂ performance is not transferable calibration.** One controlled hypoxemia study compared Apple Watch 8, Galaxy Watch 5, and Withings ScanWatch with a reference oximeter in only 18 young healthy participants (54 assessments) and reported RMS deviation ≤4% under its protocol. This is a useful validation-protocol reference, not evidence for Xiaomi's HAL channel mapping or a license to convert its raw values to percent. [Smartwatch hypoxemia comparison](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/38005550/)
9. **Wearable ECG alerts are screening workflows, not diagnosis.** The Apple Heart Study evaluated irregular-pulse notifications at scale, then used mailed ECG patches and clinical follow-up for confirmation; even a large consumer study did not equate a PPG alert with an ECG diagnosis. This supports an escalation/confirmatory pathway, not transplanting Apple's classifier to this watch. [Apple Heart Study, NEJM](https://www.nejm.org/doi/full/10.1056/NEJMoa1901183)
10. **Wrist heart-rate accuracy depends on activity and motion.** Research guidelines identify movement and activity intensity as major contributors to wrist wearable error; report activity context and signal-quality limits rather than applying one confidence threshold to rest and exercise. [npj Digital Medicine guidelines](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41746-020-0297-4)
11. **HRV readiness/stress should be individualized and contextual.** The cardiovascular wearable review emphasizes that HRV interpretation depends on metric, measurement duration, time of day, age/sex, and data quality; use standardized repeated within-person trends and avoid converting a single wrist reading into a disease label. [JACC state-of-the-art review](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10662962/)
12. **Actigraphy is not PSG-equivalent sleep staging.** A systematic review found only limited evidence for wrist actigraphy classifying sleep stages against PSG, and the included studies were small; use actigraphy for movement/sleep-wake context, not diagnostic REM/deep-stage claims without device-specific PSG validation. [Actigraphy sleep-stage systematic review](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7618292/)

### Practical algorithm decisions

- Implement HR trend and activity-context summaries only when timestamps and provenance pass; keep resting HR distinct from instantaneous HR and avoid fixed activity-independent confidence.
- Keep calories as an estimate with uncertainty and visible inputs; the literature does not support presenting wrist energy expenditure as precise clinical output.
- Current SpO₂ raw HAL events remain unusable as percent until this watch's channel mapping, units, and calibration are established. Published competitor validation cannot supply those missing device constants.
- Prioritize sleep interval/duration/regularity and data coverage. Deep/light/REM or apnea outputs require an appropriate PSG/reference cohort and device-specific held-out validation.

## Additional findings

9. **Step count is useful for activity tracking, but accuracy depends strongly on setting and population.** The INTERLIVE expert statement synthesizes 85 eligible studies and recommends validation across population, criterion measure, conditions, processing and statistical analysis; it reports examples where a wrist tracker’s MAPE was 0.7–5.2% on a treadmill versus 17.5–22.8% in free-living conditions. Product implication: expose daily steps, cadence and activity duration as observed/estimated activity features, while avoiding clinical thresholds from a single day or claiming universal accuracy. [INTERLIVE validation statement](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8273687/)
10. **Actigraphy alone is not sufficient for sleep-stage or disorder labels.** A 2024 systematic review specifically evaluating actigraphy sleep-stage performance reinforces that movement-based estimates require criterion comparison and should not be relabeled as PSG-equivalent deep/light/REM stages. Product implication: sleep interval and movement-derived wake/activity summaries may be displayed as estimates; omit stage percentages and apnea flags unless the device exposes validated additional signals and device-specific evidence. [Actigraphy sleep-stage systematic review](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/38384163/) · [Wearable sleep-staging reliability review](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/38499793/)
11. **PPG quality gating can be built as an interpretable research feature.** Song et al.'s PQR indices separately estimate high-frequency noise, baseline wander, and motion-artifact interference; the authors emphasize selecting thresholds for the application and signal source. This is suitable as a candidate raw-signal rejection explanation after the watch sample rate/channel are established, not as Xiaomi calibration or clinical validity. [PQR signal-quality indices, *Biomedical Signal Processing and Control* (2019), DOI 10.1016/j.bspc.2018.05.020](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.bspc.2018.05.020)
12. **Heart-rate recovery is protocol-sensitive.** Published validation notes that the 1–2 minute recovery response depends on exercise protocol, posture/cooldown, beta-blocker use and sampling point; cutoffs vary and diagnostic value is uncertain. If added, show only a within-person trend from consistently timed sessions with valid HR timestamps, not a disease-risk classification. [Heart rate recovery: validation and methodologic issues](https://www.jacc.org/doi/10.1016/S0735-1097%2801%2901652-7)

## Research validation plan

- **Stage 1 — software/pipeline:** synthetic boundary tests for missing, late, duplicate, stale, malformed, and reordered records; verify current-session counts and notification idempotence.
- **Stage 2 — engineering reference:** collect synchronized device/reference pairs under written protocol, consent, diverse fit/motion conditions, and holdout days. Preserve units, clocks, firmware/app build, and contact context; raw health exports remain local unless the owner authorizes a specific disclosure.
- **Stage 3 — metrics:** report sample counts and confidence intervals, bias/limits of agreement or sensitivity/specificity as appropriate, coverage/abstention rate, and false alerts per wear-hour. A 100% success/accuracy claim is not an achievable acceptance criterion; target high observability and safe abstention, then quantify validated error.
- **Stage 4 — medical interpretation:** no disease diagnosis unless intended-use clinical validation, qualified review, regulatory scope, and device-specific calibration exist. Until then, provide non-diagnostic trends and a clear clinician escalation note.

## 2026-09-27 literature check

- **Wrist PPG / heart rate:** research guidelines identify device model/firmware, sampling rate, wrist fit/contact, movement and participant factors as important to validity and reproducibility. App use: retain device/session provenance, separate rest from motion context, and abstain when the watch exposes no trustworthy sampling clock or mapping. [Nelson et al., *npj Digital Medicine* (2020), DOI 10.1038/s41746-020-0297-4](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41746-020-0297-4).
- **Wrist SpO₂:** WristO₂ is a proof-of-concept using IMU motion to identify/reject unreliable wrist oximetry readings; it is not a transferable calibration curve for this Xiaomi watch. App use: motion/contact quality can support a reject/hold state only after the PPG channel and sampling metadata are verified; do not estimate saturation from this HAL payload without device calibration. [WristO₂, IEEE International Conference on Pervasive Computing and Communications Workshops (2021), DOI 10.1109/PerComWorkshops51409.2021.9430986](https://doi.org/10.1109/PerComWorkshops51409.2021.9430986).
- **Sleep:** the adult actigraphy staging review found only eight PSG-comparison studies with heterogeneous stage grouping and metrics, preventing quantitative synthesis. App use: sleep interval/regularity and movement-derived wake are the supported near-term trend outputs; stage percentages remain estimates and not diagnostic. [Scott et al., *Journal of Sleep Research* (2024), DOI 10.1111/jsr.14143](https://doi.org/10.1111/jsr.14143).
- **Temperature:** wearable core-temperature prediction literature is limited and relies on validated model inputs and reference CBT (e.g., ingestible, rectal, or esophageal); a wrist surface-temperature reading alone is not core temperature. App use: within-device surface-temperature trend, with fit/environment context, not fever or infection classification. [Dolson et al., *Sensors* (2022), DOI 10.3390/s22197639](https://doi.org/10.3390/s22197639).
- **PPG motion artifacts:** in a six-device study, activity increased average absolute HR error by about 30% versus rest; accuracy differed by device and activity. App use: join PPG with motion context, report missingness/SQI, and validate per watch model rather than applying a generic correction. [Bent et al., *npj Digital Medicine* (2020), DOI 10.1038/s41746-020-0226-6](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41746-020-0226-6).
- **Motion-robust exercise HR:** a Samsung Research study describes band-pass preprocessing, short-time Fourier analysis, comparison of PPG and acceleration peaks, and bounded peak tracking; its reported 2.57% MAE was on its own test dataset, not this device. This suggests an offline research experiment only after reliable synchronized PPG/accelerometer sample timing is available. [Lai & Kim, *Healthcare Technology Letters* (2015), DOI 10.1049/htl.2014.0097](https://doi.org/10.1049/htl.2014.0097).
- **Wrist SpO₂ validation:** a 24-participant Apple Watch Series 6 controlled-hypoxia study found average bias 0.0%, but individual differences could be within 6% at 90–100% and up to 8% below 90% versus a Masimo reference. These results do not calibrate another watch or raw HAL channel; only device-specific paired validation can support oxygen values. [Rafl et al., *Digital Health* (2022), DOI 10.1177/20552076221132127](https://doi.org/10.1177/20552076221132127).
- **Wrist skin temperature:** the ovulation comparative study investigates longitudinal deviation against basal body temperature, supporting trend-oriented use in its specific population and task—not a general core-temperature or fever conversion. [Shilaih et al., *JMIR mHealth and uHealth* (2021), PMID 34100763](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/34100763/).

## Additional literature reviewed for current failure triage (2026-09-27)

- **PPG motion error mechanisms:** wrist displacement, changing skin deformation/perfusion, and ambient temperature can alter optical HR signal quality; this supports exposing contact/motion context and explicit rejection/coverage states rather than applying an unvalidated correction to the Xiaomi stream. [Bent et al., *npj Digital Medicine* (2020)](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7010823/).
- **HR measurement protocol:** wearable HR performance varies with activity and wrist motion; device model, fit, and context matter. Keep resting, walking, and exercise measurements stratified and validate against ECG before making device-level accuracy claims. [Nelson et al., *npj Digital Medicine* (2020)](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7320189/).
- **Smartwatch AF screening pathway:** Apple Heart Study evaluated an irregular-pulse notification workflow and followed notifications with clinical contact/ECG patch assessment; its PPV is not equivalent to diagnostic accuracy for this watch or raw ECG HAL. Implement only a research flag with clinician/reference ECG confirmation, not disease diagnosis. [Turakhia et al., *NEJM* (2019)](https://www.nejm.org/doi/full/10.1056/NEJMoa1901183).
- **Sleep staging limits:** the actigraphy-vs-PSG systematic review found only eight eligible adult studies and limited, heterogeneous staging evidence. Keep actigraphy-derived sleep/wake or regularity as estimates; do not market inferred deep/light/REM percentages as PSG-equivalent. [Scott et al., *Journal of Sleep Research* (2024)](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7618292/).
- **Current hardware boundary:** none of these studies publishes Xiaomi Watch 2 Pro MAX86178 channel mapping, ADC conversion, sampling clock, SpO₂ transfer curve, ECG lead calibration, or BioZ Ω calibration. They inform validation design and safe signal-quality handling only; they cannot unlock physical-unit or clinical gates.

## Additional primary studies and algorithm decisions (2026-09-27)

- **PPG/accelerometer sleep staging:** a 75-participant community study validated a 4-stage PPG+acceleration model against PSG, while a separate clinical cohort study of 244 adults and 48 children reported 76.4% four-class accuracy and population-dependent agreement. This supports a research sleep-stage estimate only after this watch's signals and model are validated against paired PSG; it does not justify guessing stages from movement counts or HRV alone ([Sleep 2023, PMID 37338335](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/37338335/); [Sleep 2021, PMID 34234595](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/34234595/)).
- **Consumer-watch SpO₂:** a direct comparison of common commercial watches with a clinical oximeter found substantial device-to-device error and missingness; Apple Watch Series 7 had the closest results in that study (MAE 2.2%), while Garmin Venu 2s had MAE 5.8%. Those figures are not portable to Xiaomi or MAX86178 raw HAL data; the usable algorithm is quality/missingness reporting until device-specific reference-paired validation exists ([PLOS Digital Health 2023, PMID 37437005](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/37437005/); [smartwatch hypoxemia comparison, PMID 38005550](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/38005550/)).
- **Skin-pigmentation and pulse-ox bias:** a 2024 systematic review/meta-analysis included 23 oximetry studies (59,684 participants; 197,353 paired observations) plus only four wearable pulse-rate studies (176 participants). It reinforces representative validation and uncertainty reporting rather than demographic corrections invented from this small device sample ([JMIR 2024, PMID 39388258](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/39388258/)).
- **Single-lead ECG/AF screening:** smartwatch ECG evaluations compare recordings against reference ECG in defined populations and protocols; one study enrolled patients around cardioversion, not a general unsupervised population. Apple Heart Study irregular-pulse notifications were followed by clinical contact and ECG patch confirmation. Neither supports diagnosing AF from this watch's uncalibrated ECG/PPG channel ([CVD and Heart Disorders 2022, PMID 36589758](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/36589758/); [Apple Heart Study, NEJM 2019](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/31722151/)).
- **Current implementation priority:** preserve PPG event timestamps and callback-delivery timestamps as separate clocks; mark the 8/8 missing event clocks as a timing/provenance failure and do not treat callback cadence as ADC rate. Keep the separate Health Services BPM display tied to its own sample timestamp. Missingness, raw-event presence, quality rejection and calibration absence must remain distinct states.
- **Wear OS power/API choice:** Android documents `MeasureClient` as a short-lived, rapid live-measurement callback—not a long-running subscription—and recommends `PassiveMonitoringClient` for monitoring across hours or days. The optional `HEART_RATE_5_SECONDS` exercise batching mode explicitly increases power consumption. Therefore keep `MeasureClient` scoped to the user-started scan, use passive monitoring only for the user-configured background feature, and do not promise exact periodic samples or “continuous” capture when the platform batches/limits them ([MeasureClient API](https://developer.android.com/reference/androidx/health/services/client/MeasureClient), [background monitoring guide](https://developer.android.com/health-and-fitness/health-services/monitor-background), [5-second HR batching API](https://developer.android.com/reference/kotlin/androidx/health/services/client/data/BatchingMode)).
